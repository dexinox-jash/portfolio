02 · The bet

Why I picked this fight.

Four short answers to the four questions every project should be able to answer before the first commit.

Signal

Healthcare shift scheduling is broken.

Nurse managers spend hours on the phone. Group chats are chaotic. The favor economy burns out the same people. Existing scheduling software is top-down and rigid — it doesn't handle the reality that nurses trade shifts constantly.

Filter

Vertical-specific, not horizontal.

Economic model legible in one sentence: ShiftCoin values shifts by demand, time, and desirability. Defensible by behavior — once a unit adopts it, the network effect locks in. Regulatory surface is manageable (no PHI stored).

Why now

Post-pandemic nursing shortage.

Burnout is at record highs. The favor economy is collapsing because there are fewer favors to give. Healthcare systems are desperate for retention tools. A platform that makes shift trading fair is a retention tool disguised as scheduling.

Why me

Family in healthcare.

I've watched the group-chat chaos, the guilt trips, the same three people covering every holiday. The problem isn't that nurses don't want to trade — it's that there's no fair system for valuing the trade.

01 · Problem

Nursing teams trade shifts constantly, but the process is informal, unfair, and exhausting.

Group chats favor the fastest thumb. The favor economy burns out the same people. Managers act as manual intermediaries for every swap. There's no objective valuation of shift desirability — a Friday night ICU shift is not the same as a Tuesday morning clinic shift, but they're treated identically. Unit competency gating blocks trades to unqualified nurses. A 24-hour check-in protocol ensures both parties confirm. Anti-gaming detection flags circular trading and collusion.

06 · Stakeholder views

Five people. Five opinions on what this system owes them.

Every feature is a negotiation. Every tradeoff is visible to someone. If I can't explain why it works for all five lenses, the architecture is wrong.

01

Customer (Nurse)

I want to trade my shift without begging in a group chat. I want to know what my shift is actually worth — a holiday night shift is worth more than a Tuesday morning. I want to know the person taking my shift is qualified for the unit. I want to get paid in ShiftCoin that I can spend on better shifts later. And I want it to work on my phone, offline, without installing an app from a store.

02

Business (Hospital / Unit)

Retention is cheaper than recruitment. A nurse who can trade shifts fairly is a nurse who stays. The platform replaces manager hours spent on phone calls and spreadsheets. ShiftCoin creates an internal economy that costs nothing real — it's virtual currency, not payroll. The approval matrix means managers only see the trades that actually need eyes.

03

Operations (Nurse Manager)

I need to see pending approvals, staffing alerts, and competency gaps at a glance. I need to override auto-approvals when the unit is critically short. I need audit logs for every trade in case something goes wrong. I need the system to flag suspicious patterns — circular trading, collusion, account sharing — without me watching manually.

04

Legal & Privacy

HIPAA compliance is non-negotiable. The platform stores no PHI — no patient names, no medical records, no diagnoses. It stores only scheduling data: who, when, where. The architecture makes HIPAA compliance trivial by omission. Audit logs track every trade, every approval, every ShiftCoin transaction for compliance review.

05

Growth

Network effect: one unit adopting the platform creates pressure on adjacent units to join. The playbook is unit-by-unit within a hospital, then hospital-by-hospital within a network. The ShiftCoin economy is the moat — once a unit's culture shifts to valuing shifts in coins, switching platforms means rebuilding the economy.

08 · Deep dive

The algorithm. The formula. The reason this isn't just a shift swap.

ShiftCoin is the soul of the product. Every shift has a base value, then five multipliers adjust it to reflect reality: weekend shifts cost 50% more. Holidays cost 100% more. Night shifts cost 50% more. High-demand units cost 30% more. Urgent trades (less than 7 days notice) carry a penalty. The formula is deterministic — no manager judgment, no favoritism, just math.

shiftCoinCost = baseValue
  × weekendMultiplier(1.5)
  × holidayMultiplier(2.0)
  × timeMultiplier(day 1.0 / evening 1.25 / night 1.5)
  × demandMultiplier(low 0.8 / medium 1.0 / high 1.3)
  + urgencyPenalty(<7 days notice)

A Friday night ICU shift on a holiday with high demand and 3 days notice is expensive — and that's the point. The cost signals that this shift is valuable, and the nurse who takes it earns enough ShiftCoin to trade out of three undesirable shifts later. The economy self-balances.

The 4-tier approval matrix routes trades based on three risk signals: same-unit vs cross-unit, competency match confidence, and notice period. Most trades are auto-approved in under a second. The minority that need eyes get them. Managers aren't buried in approvals — they're alerted only when something matters.

Tier 1 · Auto-approve
same-unit + qualified + 7+ days notice
→ Instant approval, no manager involved

Tier 2 · Manager notification
cross-unit or short notice
→ Awareness alert, trade proceeds

Tier 3 · Manual approval
unusual pattern or marginal competency
→ Manager must review and approve

Tier 4 · Admin override
system anomaly or flagged account
→ Escalated to administrator

The competency gate runs before ShiftCoin is deducted. The reservation lock runs before the trade is created. The approval matrix runs before the notification is sent. Every gate is in the right order.

09 · Exhibit
▸ Decision · fairness beats speed

When two nurses click the same shift in the same second

The most common failure mode in shift-trading platforms is the race condition: two nurses see an open shift, both click "Request", and the system accidentally double-allocates. The fix is a Redis reservation lock with SET NX/EX — set only if not exists, expire in 15 minutes. The interesting part isn't the Redis syntax — it's the product decision that "first click gets 15 minutes to decide" is fairer than "fastest thumb wins." The lock holds the nurse's ShiftCoin balance during the reservation, so they can't overcommit. If they don't complete the trade in 15 minutes, the lock auto-releases and the shift goes back to the pool.

BEFORE: naive first-come-first-served 
async function requestTrade(tradeOfferId, buyerId) {
  const trade = await prisma.tradeOffer
    .findUnique({ where: { id: tradeOfferId } });
  // Race condition: two requests can pass
  // this check before either writes
  if (trade.status === 'AVAILABLE') {
    await prisma.tradeOffer.update({
      where: { id: tradeOfferId },
      data: { status: 'RESERVED', buyerId }
    });
    return { success: true };
  }
  return { success: false };
}
AFTER: Redis SET NX/EX reservation lock 
async function requestTrade(tradeOfferId, buyerId) {
  const lockKey = `trade:lock:${tradeOfferId}`;
  const lock = {
    tradeOfferId, buyerId,
    reservedAt: new Date(),
    expiresAt: new Date(Date.now() + 15 * 60 * 1000),
    shiftCoinHold: trade.shiftCoinCost
  };
  // NX = only set if not exists
  // EX = expire in 900 seconds (15 min)
  const acquired = await redis.set(
    lockKey, JSON.stringify(lock),
    'NX', 'EX', 15 * 60
  );
  if (acquired !== 'OK') {
    return { success: false,
      reason: 'ALREADY_RESERVED' };
  }
  // Deduct hold from buyer balance
  await shiftCoinService.hold(buyerId,
    trade.shiftCoinCost);
  return { success: true,
    expiresAt: lock.expiresAt };
}
▸ Why this matters

Including this exhibit because the interesting part is the design choice, not the Redis command. The decision to hold ShiftCoin during the reservation (preventing overcommitment) and the 15-minute auto-expiry (preventing abandoned locks) are product decisions that happen to be implemented in Redis. The technology is trivial; the design is the story.

11 · Decisions

Five questions worth answering slowly.

Short answer first — long answer if you need it.

Q01Why a virtual currency instead of real money?+

Real money turns shift trading into a secondary labor market with payroll, tax, and regulatory complexity. Virtual currency creates the same incentive structure without the legal overhead.

ShiftCoin is an internal economy — nurses earn it by working undesirable shifts and spend it to trade out of them. The hospital doesn't pay extra; the currency redistributes desirability, not cash. Real money would require wage-and-hour analysis, tax reporting, and potentially union negotiation. Virtual currency is a behavioral nudge, not a payment system. The 5-factor valuation formula gives objective pricing to subjective preferences.

Q02Why 15 minutes for the reservation lock?+

Long enough to complete the trade form and verify competency. Short enough that an abandoned reservation doesn't block the shift for long.

The reservation lock holds the shift exclusively while the nurse verifies their competency, confirms their schedule, and submits the trade request. 5 minutes is too short for a thoughtful decision; 30 minutes is too long if the nurse walks away. 15 minutes is the editorial guess at "time to make a considered choice about taking a shift." The auto-expiry means abandoned locks don't require manual cleanup.

Q03Why 4 approval tiers instead of all-auto or all-manual?+

All-auto is dangerous for patient safety. All-manual buries managers in approvals. The 4-tier matrix routes correctly: low-risk trades happen instantly, high-risk trades get eyes.

The approval matrix uses three risk signals: same-unit vs cross-unit trade, competency match confidence, and notice period. Same-unit + qualified + 7+ days notice = auto-approve (tier 1). Cross-unit or short notice = manager notification (tier 2). Unusual pattern or marginal competency = manual approval (tier 3). System anomaly or flagged account = admin override (tier 4). The goal is manager attention only where it's actually needed. A manager who approves 50 routine trades a day will miss the one that matters.

Q04Why no PHI storage?+

Storing PHI would make the platform a HIPAA-covered entity. Not storing PHI makes compliance trivial by omission.

The platform only needs to know: which nurse, which unit, which shift time. It never needs to know which patient, which diagnosis, which procedure. By architecting PHI out of the data model from day one, the platform avoids the entire HIPAA compliance surface. If a hospital wants patient-contextual scheduling, that information stays in the hospital's EHR — the platform stores only the competency mapping, not the clinical reasoning.

Q05Why PWA instead of native mobile apps?+

Nurses use hospital devices, personal phones, and desktops. A PWA works on all three without app store approval cycles or platform-specific maintenance.

Native apps would require separate iOS and Android builds, app store review processes (1-7 days per release), and platform-specific bug tracking. A PWA installs from the browser, updates instantly on reload, works offline with service workers, and sends push notifications. For a healthcare vertical where IT departments are cautious about app installations, "open in browser, add to home screen" is dramatically lower friction. The tradeoff is reduced access to native APIs — but shift trading doesn't need the camera, accelerometer, or contacts.

13 · Reflection

What surprised me. What I'd do differently. What's next.

Surprised by

How much the vertical research mattered.

The ShiftCoin formula went through four revisions based on nurse feedback. The original formula had only three factors (day, time, demand). Nurses added holiday and urgency. The 15-minute reservation lock was originally 5 minutes — nurses said that was too short to check their family calendar. The 4-tier approval matrix was originally 2 tiers — managers said they needed more granularity. Building for a vertical you don't personally inhabit requires listening more than you talk.

Would do differently

Build a small pilot before writing the full spec.

The spec was comprehensive but theoretical. A 2-week pilot with 10 nurses would have surfaced the real pain points faster than the research phase. The competency gating, for example, turned out to be more important than the smart matching engine — but the spec weighted them equally.

What's next

Native mobile apps, calendar integration, multi-hospital.

Native mobile apps (v2.1.0) for units that prefer app-store distribution. Calendar integration (Google/Outlook) for automatic conflict detection. Advanced analytics dashboard for nurse managers. Multi-hospital support (v2.2.0). Union contract compliance checking — the most requested feature from B2B pilot conversations.
